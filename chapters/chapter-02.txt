# Chapter 2 — When Nothing Goes Smoothly

8 p.m.

Twelve hours until the placement exams.

Five days until the CrazyBand auditions closed.

Chi Zhiyang kept switching back and forth between the registration page and the recruitment post he'd published, refreshing both until his eyes started to ache. Finally, he looked up at Nan Yi, who was standing a short distance away. He was leaning against a tree, watching the entrance, his cap pulled low enough to hide most of his face. He looked just like some secret agent hired to carry out a mission.

Honestly, what was the point of waiting here? Why not go to the main entrance? And why, of all places, did Nan Yi have to hide behind the security booth? There were so many entrances. Who knew which one Qin Yiyu would show up at?

Was [[waiting by the tree for a rabbit|An idiom about waiting passively for an opportunity to fall into your lap, rather than going out and looking for it.]] really a reliable strategy?

As he switched between pages on his phone, he accidentally opened Weibo. Out of the corner of his eye, he caught a celebrity gossip account pushing some entertainment news. And wouldn't you know it: a video of Chen Yun, the young heir to Chenghong Enterprises, kissing a popular actress in an underground parking garage.

Just seeing that face and that name made him feel sick. He muttered a curse, immediately blocked the account, and prayed Nan Yi wouldn't come across something so damn unlucky.

For no particular reason, an image from the past suddenly surfaced in Chi Zhiyang's mind.

That narrow, pitch-black dead-end alley behind the north gate of their middle school. Seven or eight figures disappearing into the darkness, blocking the only way out. He'd climbed over the wall and run as fast as he could, but by the time he arrived, it was already over.

Bodies were scattered everywhere. A figure dressed in black was half-kneeling, one knee pressed hard against the chest of someone on the ground. Through the sound of ragged breathing, Chi Zhiyang could vaguely hear someone begging for mercy.

It was Chen Yun's voice.

“Xiao Yi!”

The instant he shouted, the black figure lowered his clenched fist and stood up. After a brief pause, he began walking toward him, one step at a time. His silence was heavier than the night itself. A broken streetlamp flickered on and off, its unsteady light falling across Nan Yi's face.

A blood-smeared face, utterly expressionless.

Chi Zhiyang would never forget that sight.

Bzzzz.

A mosquito with absolutely no sense of self-preservation dragged him out of his memories. It finally settled on the back of his left hand.

Smack—

One hit. Instant death. The mosquito's flattened corpse was now sitting right in the middle of the sun tattoo on the back of his hand.

Mosquitoes in September were even nastier than the ones in midsummer.

In his field of vision, Nan Yi suddenly left the old pagoda tree and started walking toward the security booth.

Chi Zhiyang's eyes followed him. He sprang to his feet.

“Holy shit. He's actually here?”

But his legs had gone numb from crouching for so long, and he couldn't catch up. All he could do was watch Nan Yi circle around the back of the security booth and head for the entrance, intercepting Qin Yiyu just as he was about to leave after returning the lost property.

It had been so long since he'd seen Qin Yiyu in the flesh that Chi Zhiyang almost felt like he'd seen a ghost.

The last time had been four years ago, at an RS livehouse show. Halfway through the performance, Qin Yiyu had gotten into a fight with the drummer and smashed a limited-edition Fender MB. It had been an absolute disaster.

Who would've thought that the rebellious, flamboyant golden boy of the past, after disappearing for so long, would show up again like this?

When Nan Yi heard Qin Yiyu greeting the security guard just now, he felt as though he were playing a video game. Restart. Load save. Over and over again.

Their conversation was almost exactly like the one he'd rehearsed in his head. The same slightly cheeky, streetwise banter, delivered in a way that was somehow still rather charming.

This was the side entrance Qin Yiyu used most often back when he attended the university. The security guard he'd been closest to was still working here, too.

When Nan Yi had seen him again a few days ago, he'd mentally catalogued every change, one by one, as if reciting a list of treasured possessions.

Thinner. His hair was still a little curly, but quite a bit longer. His skin was darker from the sun. He wasn't wearing his lip ring anymore. Had the little piercing hole closed up, too? And he had at least three new tattoos.

The most obvious change, and yet the most subtle, was his eyes.

All of it seemed to remind Nan Yi that this was the real Qin Yiyu. Not someone who existed only in his memories, nor the version who had lived inside his mind for years.

But setting those details aside, Qin Yiyu didn't seem to have changed all that much. At least, Nan Yi could still predict quite clearly what he would do and say.

Like right now, when he realised he'd been cornered.

“Listen, junior…” Just as Nan Yi had imagined, Qin Yiyu was so exasperated that he actually laughed. “You really went to a lot of trouble for this, huh?”

Nan Yi was not someone whose emotions could easily be stirred. But when he heard the word “junior,” the corner of his eye still twitched involuntarily. Even though this particular junior wasn't the same kind of junior Qin Yiyu had in mind.

In Qin Yiyu's eyes, they were strangers who'd known each other for less than a week. The only thing that had changed was that they could now add “fellow university alumnus” to the list.

Nan Yi's gaze first landed on the mole on Qin Yiyu's cheek, then drifted downward to the tattoo on his Adam's apple.

“I need you.”

He was incredibly direct.

Qin Yiyu froze for a second.

But soon enough, he laughed out loud, as though he'd just heard a joke.

Right. He remembered now. Nan Yi had said the exact same thing when they'd first met.

He'd said his band needed a lead singer who could play guitar.

*Needed.*

A word that only made him want to run away even more.

“How sincere.”

The smile gradually disappeared from Qin Yiyu's eyes, his brows settling into a straight line.

“But what's that got to do with me?”

Clearly, Nan Yi wasn't affected by his reaction. In fact, he barely seemed to have a reaction at all. He simply paused, then continued with what he'd come to say.

“There's a band competition coming up. CrazyBand. We're planning to enter, but we're still missing a guitarist.”

He handed the poster to Qin Yiyu. Right then, Chi Zhiyang finally caught up and stopped four or five metres away from them.

Nan Yi pointed toward him.

“That's our drummer. I'm the bassist. Our rehearsal room is nearby. I know you're not interested right now, but you could at least come and watch us rehearse first, if you have the time.”

Qin Yiyu glanced at the poster, then shifted his gaze to the drummer with the bleached-white hair, before looking back at Nan Yi. He nearly burst out laughing.

What a strange person.

If you called him single-minded, he was actually pretty clever. He'd figured Qin Yiyu out almost completely. But if you called him cunning, his persuasion skills were absolutely terrible. He didn't even know how to talk someone into joining a band.

And there was something else Qin Yiyu had noticed. They'd crossed paths three times now, and he still couldn't form a clear impression of this person's face. Was it because he was always wearing a cap that hid his eyebrows and eyes? It left Qin Yiyu with no choice but to stare at his lips while he spoke.

Though, to be fair, he did have a rather nice lip shape. It would suit a lip piercing.

Wait. That wasn't the point.

Qin Yiyu shook off those strange thoughts and dragged himself back to the matter at hand.

“I really don't have anywhere to be. But sorry, I'm trash. I'm not interested in drummers, bassists, or bands.”

He crumpled the poster into a ball and brushed past Nan Yi's shoulder. His voice was lazy and dismissive.

“Even trash has the right to stay in the garbage bin instead of being recycled.”

And with that, Qin Yiyu left them with nothing but his back.

“Why bother starting a band? Just focus on your studies, freshman.”

Chi Zhiyang felt like there was no hope at all.

Years ago, Qin Yiyu's negative reputation had become just as well-known as his music: neurotic, stubborn, narcissistic, arrogant, unpredictable, constantly putting down his bandmates, and impossible to work with.

He was like a hurricane. He would suddenly appear, sweep everything away, then vanish without a trace at some inexplicable moment, leaving nothing behind but wreckage.

Nobody knew why he'd disappeared. Nobody knew. Not even Nan Yi.

Nan Yi had gone to this university looking for Qin Yiyu, but all he'd found out was that Qin Yiyu had taken a leave of absence.

When he saw the band again later, they'd already replaced both their lead singer and guitarist. Like so many bands that went through lineup changes, they'd simply carried on.

But nobody could erase the traces Qin Yiyu had left behind. The heights he'd taken them to, the wreckage he'd left in his wake, his unmistakable vocal style and songwriting, his devoted fans and the people who couldn't stand him…

It was all like a branding iron heated to its limit, leaving behind a mark that would never fade.

Perhaps Qin Yiyu himself was simply too dangerous to be squeezed into any band.

Even now, the comment section under their debut single still had a highly upvoted comment that had caused an absolute storm of controversy:

“Wherever Qin Yiyu shows up, his ‘curse of the spotlight’ follows.”

Chi Zhiyang had said it ages ago: someone like that could never be won over. What once-in-a-generation genius would willingly return to the [[newbie village|A gaming reference to the beginner area where new players start out. Here, it means going back to square one and helping inexperienced newcomers.]] just to lead a bunch of rookies? And on top of that, this was Qin Yiyu they were talking about, a man who didn't give a damn about anything. Putting his name next to a sentence like that was practically a joke.

But Nan Yi had never listened.

“Seriously, what's wrong with that guy?! I…” Remembering just how fixated Nan Yi was on Qin Yiyu, Chi Zhiyang swallowed the curse he was about to say. “Maybe we should just stop trying to recruit him!”

Nan Yi didn't look particularly discouraged. There was just a little confusion in his eyes.

He stood there for a while, then turned back toward the security booth to retrieve his card holder. He didn't really respond to Chi Zhiyang's suggestion.

“Let's head back for now. Don't you have an early class tomorrow?”

“Okay.” Chi Zhiyang sighed. “It's fine. It's not like he's the only person we can recruit.”

As soon as he said it, the words felt pointless. He was terrible at comforting people, and Nan Yi didn't need comforting anyway. So he changed the subject.

“Are you still going to work at 029 tonight? It's already so late, and you've got an exam tomorrow. You should at least go back and do some revision.”

029 was a large party venue near Nan Yi's university. The owner was from Xi'an, so she'd named the place after the city's telephone area code.

“No need.” Nan Yi didn't bother checking the contents of his card holder. He knew everything was still there, so he simply tossed it into his bag. “I switched my shift to tomorrow afternoon. I've got no classes after the exam.”

Good grief. He'd even rearranged his part-time shift. He'd really been planning to spend the entire night waiting around here.

Chi Zhiyang scratched his head.

He'd originally wanted to come clean about the musician recruitment post he'd put up earlier. But clearly, at this point, Nan Yi didn't want anyone except Qin Yiyu.

“Fine. See you at the rehearsal room tomorrow.” Chi Zhiyang rolled his arms around a little. “I think I've been practising too much lately. My arm hurts so badly I can barely lift it. I need to go home and slap on a pain-relief patch. You should stop practising tonight, too. Get some rest.”

“Mm.”

As though nothing had happened, Nan Yi patted Chi Zhiyang on the shoulder. He even gave him a smile.

He had four canine teeth that were longer and sharper than those of an ordinary person. Combined with his slightly upturned eyes, the whites showing faintly beneath them, and his unusually pale irises, they gave him an untamed, almost feral look.

People with teeth like that seemed to have dimples, too. Nan Yi had one, a very faint one on his right cheek that only appeared when he smiled.

“We still have time. Don't worry.”

The dormitory was quite far away, so Nan Yi rode his bike back. As he passed a pedestrian crossing, he experienced that same auditory hallucination again: the sound of a tremendous impact mixed with the wail of an ambulance.

After all these years, he still hadn't gotten used to it.

So he put on his headphones. By coincidence, the first song that played was one of Qin Yiyu's old tracks.

Little by little, Qin Yiyu's voice drowned out the phantom noise. By the time Nan Yi was close to his dormitory, it had finally disappeared.

Nan Yi couldn't understand it.

How could Qin Yiyu just stop singing?

Observing and analysing had become second nature to him. Behind every person's every action, there was a motive and a logic. Everything could be broken down and examined. The more thoroughly and clearly he understood something, the easier it was to control.

He wanted to piece together a complete picture of Qin Yiyu, like putting together a jigsaw puzzle. Then he would be able to persuade him.

But perhaps there was one crucial piece of information missing. That was why, no matter how hard he tried, he still couldn't get what he wanted.

He would figure out what it was eventually.

***

The following afternoon, Qin Yiyu was wandering aimlessly along Xisi Street, eating an ice cream. He'd just reached Xishiku Church when a message from Zhou Huai came through.

**[Huai: Didn't you mention that competition the other day? CrazyBand, right? I asked around. Seems pretty lively.**

**[Huai: Apparently, one of the investors behind it is Chenghong Entertainment. So even getting through to the audition recording stage pays, and the prize money is even bigger. Even third place gets a seven-figure prize. The champion gets an absolutely ridiculous cash prize, plus the whole band gets signed to ZIA, one of Chenghong's major labels. They'll even get a headline slot at one of the three biggest music festivals. Pretty much the same treatment you got back then.]**

Qin Yiyu typed with the ice cream stick still between his teeth.

**[Fish: How many competitions like this have there been? Don't they all end up flopping?]**

**[Huai: Okay, but hear me out. An older friend of mine has two livehouses in Beijing. One of them has been signed on by the organisers as an audition venue. It's that place where you had your first performance. Dream Island. Remember?]**

**[Fish: Yang Xi, right? Yeah, I remember. He's like you, isn't he? Seriously, do you gays have some kind of special magnetic field or something? Like the sparrows at Beihai Park, always gathering in flocks.]**

**[Huai: Watch your mouth, you hopeless straight guy.]**

Qin Yiyu sent back a cheeky yellow-face meme, disgusting Zhou Huai enough to get him to return to the actual topic.

**[Huai: …]**

**[Huai: He said the format this time is completely different from previous competitions. Lots of new gimmicks. Maybe they'll actually manage to breathe some life into the scene.]**

**[Huai: Someone's definitely going to cover your songs.]**

**[Fish: Don't even. How can they be my songs? Careful, or you'll get a lawyer's letter.]**

He wasn't like he had been right after the incident anymore. These days, Qin Yiyu could talk about all that ugly business calmly. He could even joke about it with Zhou Huai.

It wasn't as though he cared about anything anymore.

**[Huai: Fuck that. They're your songs!]**

The weather was lovely. A gentle breeze swept through his hair. The church in the distance had begun its mass, and the choir's voices drifted over, clear and peaceful.

Qin Yiyu narrowed his eyes, then simply lay down flat on the ground.

Passers-by turned to stare, but he couldn't care less. He just wanted to lie there like a dead fish and let the blazing sun bake him from head to toe.

An elderly street cleaner nearby saw him and kindly called out, “Young man, are you alright?”

With his eyes still closed, Qin Yiyu shouted back as though he were singing a folk song.

“I'm fine! Don't worry about me! I'm just sick in the head!”

The old man's broom slipped from his hands and clattered to the ground.

The sunlight was dazzling. For one brief second, surrounded by all the noise and bustle, Qin Yiyu felt as though he'd travelled back in time. It reminded him of lying on the rooftop in high school. It felt almost exactly the same.

But then his phone vibrated again, shattering that familiar feeling.

He knew it.

All these sudden messages, all that beating around the bush. Zhou Huai was definitely building up to something else.

**[Huai: I'm telling you, be careful. Don't let someone use you again. That handsome kid went to an awful lot of trouble to find you. You can't seriously think you don't know why, can you? If he manages to get a former member of a hugely popular band, someone who's stirred up endless controversy and still has a massive following, to join his little group, then whether they win or not, the publicity alone will be insane. With your popularity, your whole reputation, who wouldn't want to ride your coattails for a bit?]**

Zhou Huai hadn't originally wanted to put it so bluntly.

But he couldn't bear to watch his friend repeat the same mistakes, only to get tangled up with another bloodsucker.

At the top of the chat, the notification **[The other person is typing…]** remained on the screen.

Well, looks like that hit a nerve.

He was probably busy writing a whole essay agreeing with him and complaining about the kid.

But in the end, Zhou Huai received only one message.

**[Fish: You're right. I'm fucking awesome.]**

*Actually, I might be pretty easy to fool. He already managed to trick me into going back to the university. At this rate, he might as well ask me to go study in the library with him.*

Delete. Delete.

**[Huh? He's not that bad, is he? I think he's pretty stubborn. He's just a kid, a freshman. What could he possibly know? At most, he could fool a few girls.]**

*Actually, he could probably fool a whole crowd of girls.*
