  2: `Chapter 2: Nothing Ever Goes Smoothly

At eight that evening.

Twelve hours until the placement exam.

Five days until submissions for the CrazyBand auditions closed.

Chi Zhiyang kept switching back and forth between the registration page and his recruitment post, refreshing both until his eyes began to ache. Only then did he finally look up at Nan Yi, who was standing some distance away.

He was leaning against a tree, watching the entrance with most of his face hidden beneath his cap. He looked less like a college student and more like some hired operative waiting to carry out a covert mission.

Seriously, what was the point of waiting here? Why not go to the main entrance? And why on earth had he chosen to lurk behind the security booth? There were so many entrances around campus. Who knew which one Qin Yiyu would show up at?

Was sitting around waiting for the rabbit to run into a tree stump really a viable strategy?

As he switched between pages on his phone, he accidentally opened Weibo. An entertainment gossip account had just posted a story. What a coincidence: a video of Chen Yun, the heir to Chenghong Enterprises, caught kissing a popular actress in an underground parking garage.

The moment Chi Zhiyang saw that face and that name, his stomach turned. He muttered a curse under his breath, immediately blocked the account, and silently prayed that Nan Yi wouldn't come across something so damn unpleasant.

For no particular reason, a scene from the past suddenly surfaced in his mind.

The narrow, pitch-black alley behind the north gate of their middle school. Seven or eight figures swallowed by the darkness, blocking off any chance of escape.

He'd climbed over the wall and run as fast as he could, but by the time he got there, it was already over.

Bodies were sprawled across the ground. A figure dressed in black was kneeling over someone, one knee pressing hard against the person's chest.

Amid the ragged breathing, Chi Zhiyang could just make out someone begging for mercy.

It was Chen Yun's voice.

"Xiao Yi!"

The instant he shouted, the black-clad figure lowered his clenched fist and stood up. He paused for a moment before slowly walking toward him, one step at a time.

His silence was heavier than the night itself.

The broken streetlight flickered overhead, its intermittent glow illuminating Nan Yi's face.

A blood-smeared face, utterly devoid of expression.

Chi Zhiyang would never forget that sight.

Bzzzz.

An inconsiderate mosquito dragged him out of his memories, eventually settling on the back of his left hand.

Smack!

One clean kill.

The mosquito's remains were flattened right in the middle of the sun tattoo on his hand.

September mosquitoes were even nastier than the ones in midsummer.

In his peripheral vision, Nan Yi suddenly left the old pagoda tree and started walking toward the security booth.

Chi Zhiyang followed him with his eyes, then shot to his feet.

"No fucking way. He actually showed up?"

Unfortunately, his legs had gone numb from crouching for so long. He couldn't catch up. All he could do was watch as Nan Yi circled around the back of the security booth and intercepted Qin Yiyu just as he was leaving after returning the lost property.

It had been so long since he'd seen Qin Yiyu looking like an actual, living person that Chi Zhiyang almost felt as though he'd traveled back in time.

The last time he'd seen him was four years ago, at one of RS's livehouse shows. Halfway through the performance, Qin Yiyu had gotten into a fight with the drummer and smashed a limited-edition Fender MB guitar. The whole thing had been an absolute disaster.

Who would've thought that the rebellious, arrogant golden boy of those days, after disappearing for so long, would resurface like this?

When Nan Yi had heard Qin Yiyu greeting the security guard earlier, he'd felt as though he were playing a video game.

Restart. Reload save. Over and over again.

Because their conversation had played out almost exactly as he'd rehearsed it in his head. Even the slightly cheeky, irreverent banter was exactly as he'd imagined: a little roguish, but somehow still effortlessly charming.

This was the side entrance Qin Yiyu used most often back when he attended the university. Even the security guard he'd been closest to was still working here.

When he'd seen Qin Yiyu again a few days ago, Nan Yi had mentally taken stock of all the changes, going through them one by one as though reciting a familiar list.

Thinner. His hair was still a little curly, but considerably longer. His skin was darker from the sun. He wasn't wearing his lip ring anymore. Had the piercing closed up, too? He had at least three new tattoos.

The most obvious change, and yet the most subtle, was his eyes.

Everything seemed to remind Nan Yi that this was the real Qin Yiyu. Not someone who existed only in his memories, nor the version he'd carried around in his head for all these years.

And yet, setting aside those little details, Qin Yiyu didn't seem to have changed all that much.

At least, Nan Yi could still predict exactly what he would do and say.

For example, what he would say now, having discovered that someone had deliberately cornered him here.

"Look, junior..."

Just as Nan Yi had imagined, Qin Yiyu actually laughed in disbelief.

"You really went all out for this, didn't you?"

Nan Yi was not someone whose emotions could easily be stirred. But at the word junior, the corner of his eye twitched involuntarily.

Even if, technically speaking, he wasn't that kind of junior.

In Qin Yiyu's eyes, they were practically strangers who'd met less than a week ago. The only thing that had changed since then was that they could now add fellow university alumnus to the list.

Nan Yi's gaze first settled on the mole on Qin Yiyu's cheek, then drifted downward, stopping at the tattoo on his Adam's apple.

"I need you."

He said it with complete bluntness.

Qin Yiyu froze for a second.

Then, as though he'd just heard the funniest joke in the world, he burst out laughing.

Right. He remembered now. Nan Yi had said the exact same thing when they'd first met.

He'd said his band needed a lead vocalist who could play guitar.

Need.

What a word. One that only made Qin Yiyu want to run even further in the opposite direction.

"Well, aren't you sincere?"

The smile gradually faded from Qin Yiyu's eyes, his arched brows settling into a straight line.

"But what does that have to do with me?"

Clearly, Nan Yi wasn't affected in the slightest. Not that he seemed to have much of an emotional reaction to anything in the first place. He paused briefly, then continued with what he'd come to say.

"There's a band competition coming up. CrazyBand. We're planning to enter, but we're still short a guitarist."

He handed Qin Yiyu a flyer.

By then, Chi Zhiyang had finally caught up and was standing about four or five meters away.

Nan Yi gestured toward him.

"He's our drummer. I'm the bassist. Our rehearsal room is nearby. I know you're probably not interested right now, but you could at least come by and watch us practice. If you're not in a hurry, that is."

Qin Yiyu glanced at the flyer, then shifted his gaze toward the white-haired drummer, before finally looking back at Nan Yi.

He almost laughed.

What a strange guy.

You could call him single-minded, but he was actually pretty clever. He seemed to have figured Qin Yiyu out completely.

But if he was so clever, why was he so terrible at persuading people? He couldn't even put together a decent pitch to recruit a new band member.

And there was something else Qin Yiyu had noticed. This was already their third encounter, yet he still couldn't get a clear impression of the guy's face.

Was it because he kept wearing that cap, hiding his eyebrows and eyes? It meant Qin Yiyu could only watch his mouth when he spoke.

Though, to be fair, he did have rather nice lips. The sort that would look good with a lip piercing.

Wait. What was he thinking?

Qin Yiyu shook off the strange thoughts and dragged himself back to the matter at hand.

"Actually, I'm not in a hurry. But sorry, I'm a lost cause. I have absolutely no interest in drummers, bassists, or bands."

He crumpled the flyer into a ball, brushed past Nan Yi's shoulder, and spoke in a lazy, offhand tone.

"Even trash has the right to stay in the dumpster instead of being recycled."

And with that, Qin Yiyu walked away, leaving Nan Yi with nothing but his retreating back.

"Why bother starting a band? Focus on your studies, freshman."

Chi Zhiyang genuinely thought there was no hope.

Qin Yiyu's reputation had become just as notorious as his music years ago. Neurotic. Stubborn. Narcissistic. Arrogant. Unpredictable. Controlling toward his bandmates. Impossible to work with.

He was like a hurricane. He would appear without warning, sweep through everything in his path, then vanish just as suddenly, leaving nothing but destruction behind.

No one knew why he'd disappeared. No one had ever found out.

Not even Nan Yi.

He'd gone to the university looking for Qin Yiyu once, only to learn that he'd taken a leave of absence.

By the time he saw the band again, they'd already replaced both their lead vocalist and guitarist, carrying on much like any other band that had undergone a lineup change.

But nobody could erase the traces Qin Yiyu had left behind.

The heights he'd brought them to. The wreckage he'd left behind. His unmistakable vocal style and songwriting. The devoted fans who worshipped him, and the people who couldn't stand the sight of him...

Every last trace was like a branding iron heated white-hot, leaving a mark that would never fade.

Perhaps Qin Yiyu himself was simply too dangerous to fit into any band.

Even now, the comment section under their debut single still had one highly upvoted comment that never failed to stir up a storm.

[Wherever Qin Yiyu goes, his "curse of the spotlight" follows.]

Chi Zhiyang had said it countless times before. There was no way anyone could recruit someone like him.

What world-renowned genius would willingly return to the beginner's village just to carry a bunch of newbies through their first quests?

And this was Qin Yiyu, of all people.

Putting his name and that sentence together was practically a joke.

But Nan Yi had never listened.

"Seriously, what's wrong with that guy?! I..."

Thinking about just how stubborn Nan Yi was when it came to Qin Yiyu, Chi Zhiyang swallowed the curse he'd been about to let out.

"Maybe we should just stop trying to recruit him."

Nan Yi didn't look particularly discouraged. There was just a hint of confusion in his eyes.

After standing there for a while, he turned around and went back to the security booth to retrieve his cardholder. He didn't seem particularly bothered by Chi Zhiyang's suggestion.

"Let's head back for now. Don't we have an early class tomorrow?"

"Fine." Chi Zhiyang sighed. "It's okay. It's not like he's our only option."

But the moment he said it, he realized how pointless it sounded. He was terrible at comforting people, and Nan Yi didn't really need comforting anyway.

So he changed the subject.

"Are you still working at 029 tonight? It's already so late, and you've got an exam tomorrow. You should at least go home and do some revision."

029 was a large party venue near Nan Yi's university. The owner was from Xi'an, so she'd simply named the place after the city's telephone area code.

"No need." Nan Yi didn't bother checking the contents of his cardholder. He knew everything was there, so he simply tossed it into his bag.

"I switched my shift to tomorrow afternoon. I've got no classes after the exam."

Holy shit.

He'd even rearranged his work schedule. So he really had been prepared to spend the entire night waiting here for Qin Yiyu.

Chi Zhiyang scratched his head.

He'd originally wanted to come clean about the recruitment post he'd put up looking for other musicians. But it was pretty obvious that, at this point, Nan Yi wasn't interested in anyone except Qin Yiyu.

"All right, then. See you at the rehearsal room tomorrow."

Chi Zhiyang rolled his shoulders.

"I think I've been overdoing it lately. My arm hurts so much I can barely lift it. I need to go home and slap on a pain relief patch. You should take it easy, too. No more practicing tonight. Get some sleep."

"Mm."

As though nothing had happened, Nan Yi patted Chi Zhiyang on the shoulder. He even gave him a smile.

He had four canine teeth that were longer and sharper than most people's. Combined with his slightly upturned eyes, where a sliver of white was always visible beneath his irises, and his unusually light-colored eyes, they gave him an untamed, almost feral look.

People with teeth like his seemed to come with dimples as a package deal. Nan Yi had one, too, a faint little dimple on his right cheek that only appeared when he smiled.

"We still have time. Don't worry."

The dormitory was a long way away, so Nan Yi rode his bicycle home.

As he passed a pedestrian crossing, he heard it again.

A phantom sound.

The deafening crash of an impact, mixed with the wail of an ambulance.

After all these years, he still hadn't gotten used to it.

He put on his headphones. By sheer coincidence, the first song that played was one of Qin Yiyu's old tracks.

Little by little, Qin Yiyu's voice drowned out the phantom noise. By the time Nan Yi reached his dormitory, it had finally disappeared.

Nan Yi couldn't understand.

How could Qin Yiyu simply stop singing?

Observing and analyzing people had become second nature to him. Every action had a motive, a logic behind it. Anything could be broken down and examined. The more thoroughly he understood someone, the more clearly he could predict their behavior, and the easier they were to keep under control.

He wanted to piece Qin Yiyu together like a jigsaw puzzle, until he could see the complete picture. Then he'd be able to convince him.

But perhaps there was one crucial piece missing. Something he hadn't managed to uncover, which was why he'd failed every time.

He would figure it out eventually.

He wasn't like he used to be, back when everything had first gone wrong.

Qin Yiyu narrowed his eyes, then simply flopped down flat on the ground.

Zhou Huai was probably busy composing a whole essay in response, ready to rant right alongside him.
